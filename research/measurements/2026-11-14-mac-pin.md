# Mac: keeping the mapped weights in memory under memory pressure

Issue #108. Follows [2026-11-14-mac-ab-order.md](2026-11-14-mac-ab-order.md#results): at a memory level of 3 to 9 %
macOS drops clean pages of the mapped weights, the GPU waits for their page-in, and decode runs about 5 % slower for
minutes at a time. Product rule "work first" (Tona beside the user's work): pinning may not take memory the user needs
without a bound; a default that holds memory is the maintainer's decision. This probe brings the numbers.

## Plan (written before the runs)

**Change under test.** `TONA_METAL_PIN` (probe switch, empty = off = today), applied once after the weights are
mapped (`Weights::pin`):

- `rset`: one `MTLResidencySet` with every weight buffer, added to the command queue, `requestResidency` (macOS 15).
- `mlock`: `mlock` of every weight window (20.5 GB for DM-T; `vm.user_wire_limit` 27.1 GB on this machine).
- `mlock-dense`: `mlock` of the tensors that are not routed experts only (4.4 GB): attention, shared expert, norms,
  embedding and head.

The n-gram table (28.8 GB, part 2 of DM-T, rows read by the CPU) stays unpinned in every arm: larger than the wire
limit. `tona-bench` now prints the major faults (`getrusage`) of the whole run: pages read back from disk.

**Prior art.** llama.cpp (`ggml-metal-device.m`, local checkout d9ee007) puts every no-copy buffer of a mapped model
into a residency set on macOS 15 and keeps it resident with a heartbeat (`requestResidency` every 5 ms) until 3
minutes after the last graph (`GGML_METAL_RESIDENCY_KEEP_ALIVE_S`), then lets it go; `GGML_METAL_NO_RESIDENCY`
turns it off. llama.cpp's `--mlock` wires the whole mapping. Neither bounds the memory held.

**Smoke test (no pressure, one round, not a result).** Pinned bytes 20.54, 20.54 and 4.43 GB; major faults 1946
(off), 10 (`rset`), 3 (`mlock`), 3 (`mlock-dense`): pinning brings the pages in at load.

**Pressure.** `work/balloon.py N` holds N GiB of written anonymous memory until killed, started before the model
loads (it stands for the user's programs).

**Runs.**

1. Calibration (off only): balloon 0, 6, 9, 12 GiB, `tona-bench DMT -b 4 -p 512 -d 128 -r 3 --ctx 4096`, with the
   load sampler. Picks the balloon size at which the off arm pages in (memory level ≤ 9 %, as in the A/A), without
   swapping the machine to a stall. ~8 min.
2. Main run: no balloon and the chosen balloon size; arms off, `rset`, `mlock`, `mlock-dense`; order off, rset,
   mlock, dense, dense, mlock, rset, off per pressure level (16 runs, ~30 min). Per run: pass median, major faults
   of the bench, and from the sampler page-ins, swap-ins, compressions and the lowest memory level of the machine
   (what pinning does to the rest of the system).

**Expectation.**

- No balloon: all arms within ±0.5 ms per pass of each other (no page-ins to save); faults off > pinned arms.
- Balloon: off 3 to 6 % slower than without balloon, with GB of page-ins (the A/A's slow state); `rset` and `mlock`
  within 1 % of their no-balloon speed with almost no major faults from the weights; the rest of the system pays: more
  compressions and swap-ins than with off, a lower memory level. `mlock-dense` between off and full: the routed
  experts (16 GB) are most of the weights, and most of an expert matrix is read only when routed to.
- If `rset` does not protect (a residency set may only map the pages for the GPU, not wire them), that is a finding.

**Two options for the decision (worked out after the runs):** (a) off as today; (b) pin with a budget: hold up to the
memory the installer asked the user to leave for Tona, release under pressure (memory level below a threshold) or
after idle, as llama.cpp's keep-alive does.

**Active tooling.** `TONA_METAL_PIN`, the balloon, the sampler (60 ms per 2 s); no other `TONA_*` beyond the
defaults; no profile mode.

## Calibration: memory pressure alone does not slow decode

Conditions: before ok; after calibration 1 ok, after 2 warning (6.9 % other CPU), after 3 warning (load average 6.2
from the balloon's own paging); raw output in [2026-11-14-mac-pin.log](2026-11-14-mac-pin.log). Off arm only,
`tona-bench DMT -b 4 -p 512 -r 3 --ctx 4096` (`-d 128` in 1, `-d 384` in 2 and 3); machine values from the sampler
during each run:

| Balloon | Pass ms | Bench major faults | Page-ins | Swap-ins | Compressed | Memory level min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (1) | 84.23 | 7390 | 0.4 GB | 0 | 0 | 20 |
| 6 / 9 / 12 GiB of zero pages (1) | 84.33 / 84.27 / 84.38 | 12149 / 14547 / 18050 | 3.4 / 5.3 / 7.5 GB | 0 | 4.8 / 6.6 / 9.4 GB | 17 to 18 |
| none (2) | 83.90 | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 17 |
| 10 / 13 / 16 GiB random, static (2) | 83.30 / 83.97 / 82.92 | 20888 / 20796 / 23136 | 9.2 / 10.4 / 12.4 GB | 0.2 to 0.8 GB | 8 to 17 GB | 6 to 7 |
| 10 / 13 GiB random, every page touched each 10 s (3) | 83.62 / 83.23 | 22501 / 28280 | 12.5 / 13.8 GB | 12.5 / 12.7 GB | 37 / 47 GB | 8 |

Calibration 1 was no pressure at all (zero pages compress to nothing; balloon then filled with random bytes). In 2
and 3 the machine reached the memory level of the A/A's slow starts and paged in 9 to 14 GB, mostly while the model
loaded (prefill of round 1 146 to 189 tok/s against 200), yet **the decode pass stayed within ±0.7 ms of the run
without balloon**. macOS swaps the balloon out and keeps the weights a running decode keeps touching.

**Finding: memory pressure alone does not make the slow state.** The A/A's page-ins go with the slow starts, but a
balloon that reproduces the page-ins, the compressions and the memory level does not reproduce the slowdown. The page-
ins there may be a companion of the cause (other programs starting or working, which also use the GPU, the memory
bandwidth or the CPU) rather than the cause. In the A/A the GPU utilization (ours included) was 96.6 to 97.1 % in
fast starts and 93.4 to 95.8 % in slow ones: the GPU waits more in slow starts, for something not yet named.

**Consequence for the plan.** The main run (pin arms under a balloon) would compare arms where the off arm is not
slow: it cannot show a gain. Not run as planned. The pin switch itself works (major faults 1946 → 3 to 10 in the
smoke test).

Limit of the calibration: `tona-bench` is not the server path (no MTP head, context 4096 instead of 32768, no
requests over HTTP); the server could still be hit where the bench is not.

## Server A/B plan: off against `mlock`, natural conditions

The direct test of whether pinning removes the slow state where it was seen: `scripts/ab.py --workload set`, DM-T +
MTP-164, same build, A defaults, B `TONA_METAL_PIN=mlock` (the strongest pin; 22.8 GB with the head), `--fixed-rounds
--max-rounds 6`, `--load-log` on. About 60 min. Expectation: if page-ins of the weights are the cause, B has no slow
starts (rate ≥ 1.03 of the median throughout) while A keeps its blocks, so B ahead by 0 to +5 % depending on how many
slow blocks fall into the hour, with page-ins of B's server near zero; if B shows slow starts too, pinning does not
help and the probe ends. For the rest of the system: B holds 22.8 GB wired, so other programs' compressions and
swap-ins can only rise (the load log shows how much).

## Server A/B result: pinning does not remove the slow state

Conditions: before warning (other programs 5.0 % of all CPU threads, load average 3.13), after "do not measure" (GPU
96 % busy: the reading lags 1 to 2 min, it is the run's own server) and 20 s later ok; log in
[2026-11-14-mac-pin.log](2026-11-14-mac-pin.log). Report and load table:
[2026-11-14-mac-pin-server-ab.md](2026-11-14-mac-pin-server-ab.md). Every B server printed `Metal: mlock pinned 21.49
GB` (12 of 12 starts).

**Finding: outside the expectation, the probe ends with no default change.** With every weight wired, B still has
slow starts (37.0 and 36.7 tok/s code), and as in the A/A they go with a lower share of GPU time for us (87 to 91 %
against 94 to 95 % in fast starts) and with page-ins of the machine. Those page-ins cannot be our weights (wired), so
in the A/A, too, they were a companion of the slow state, not its cause; with the calibration (pressure alone does not
slow the bench) the weight page-in hypothesis is refuted twice. Round 6 ran during a backup (system CPU 64 to 70 % of
one core) and has the largest gap.

Open, not tested: (1) the n-gram table (28.8 GB, read by the CPU every token, never pinned) loses its pages under
pressure; wiring 21.5 GB of weights leaves it less room, which would fit B being 1.5 % behind rather than level. (2)
What makes the GPU wait in slow starts (memory bandwidth taken by others, the GPU clock) needs root to read
(`powermetrics`) and is not seen.

**For the decision (work first):** nothing to decide. Option (b), pin with a budget, has no measured gain to pay for
the memory it holds; `TONA_METAL_PIN` stays a probe switch, off. Principle 8: the gain was not discarded, it was not
there (B-A -1.5 % [-3.3, +0.4]); research: llama.cpp's residency sets guard against idle ("keep the residency sets
wired for that amount of time to avoid being collected by the OS", `ggml-metal-device.m`, 3 min after the last
graph), not against pressure during work, and our server has no idle between requests (≤ 1 s, A/A).

**Transfer to the PC: none.** On Linux the dense weights live in VRAM and their mapped pages are dropped after upload
(`engine/gpu/mega_init.cpp`); only host parts (experts outside VRAM, the n-gram table) could page, and the PC server
already reports its pinned GB. Nothing changes there.
