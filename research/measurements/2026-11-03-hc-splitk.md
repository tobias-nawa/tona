# gr.down and gr.up with a split sum (split-K), prototype (#37)

Date: 2026-11-03. Branch `37-hc-splitk` (from main 15bc6be). Build `work/build/w37`.

## Plan: probe (block 1)

Question: how many microseconds per call does it save to cut the sum of a row of `gr.down` (320 rows x 10,240
columns, Q8_0) and of `gr.up` (10,240 rows x 320 columns) into parts that run on different waves (or, for `gr.up`, on
more lanes), before any change to the megakernel?

Decision rule from the ticket: if the probe saves less than 3 us per call, stop here. 97 calls per pass x 3 us =
0.29 ms of a 23.7 ms pass (1.2 % decode); below that the work is not worth the risk in the megakernel.

Scope: `ie-wide-probe --hc --splitk` (new), all under `/var/tmp/claude/gpu.lock`, no build during a run, `IE_DIAG=1`.
- The kernel is the megakernel's own `row_q8<8, NB>` (same code, same G = 2 loads), launched as 32 blocks x 16 waves
  like `k_mega`, weights in 64 copies used in turn (3.5 MB each, 223 MB in total, above the Infinity Cache), so the
  weights are cold as in a pass. Median of 9 timings of 64 launches each (the ~2 us launch is included, as in
  2026-10-25-hc-matvec.md).
- Matrices: 320 x 10,240 with P = 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10 parts (L = 32 lanes per unit; unit = row x part; 320 sub-blocks per
  row divide by these P); 10,240 x 320 with P x L from (1, 2) (today) to (10, 2) (10 sub-blocks per row).
- nb = 1 and 4. `gr.down` with x from memory (what the pass does at nb = 1: only `gr.up` stages x in LDS) and, for
  comparison with 10-25, x in LDS; `gr.up` with x in LDS.
- Three separate process runs per variant for the spread (the medians inside a run are over 9 timings).
- Deviation: the partial sums added in the fixed order p = 0 .. P-1, against the P = 1 sum from `k_probe` of the same
  weights (largest difference divided by the largest |y|, and the number of results that differ in any bit). The
  row (P = 1, L = today) must be bit-identical to `k_probe` (check of the new kernel).

Expectation (tolerance +-15 %):
- Today (P = 1): 22.6 us (320 x 10,240) and 20.9 us (10,240 x 320) at nb = 1, cold (source: 2026-10-25-hc-matvec.md;
  the kernel has not changed since, only G = 2 and the step profile of 11-02).
- Why smaller is possible: per lane the chain is 10 sub-blocks (5 rounds of G = 2) for `gr.down` on 320 of 512 waves.
  With P = 2 the chain is 5 sub-blocks (3 rounds) but 640 units on 512 waves, so 128 waves run two units; with P = 4
  1,280 units of 80 sub-blocks (2.5 per lane), 2 or 3 units per wave. My guess for the best P is a gain of 3 to 6 us
  per call; **ungeprueft**, the probe decides.
- Deviation: float reassociation of at most 320 terms, expected 1e-7 to 1e-6 of max |y|. Anything bigger is a finding.

Why this size: the two matrices are the ones of the ticket and `ie-wide-probe --hc` is the tool the 10-25 numbers come
from, so the comparison needs no new baseline logic. A probe launch is 15 ms per variant; there is no cheaper way to
tell whether a longer pass is worth building.

Active tooling: `IE_DIAG=1`, `IE_HC_NC` default 64 (cold). No profiler, no `IE_MEGA_PROF` (the megakernel is not
involved). No rate from this block goes into BENCHMARKS.md.

## Results: probe (block 1)

Three runs of `ie-wide-probe --hc --splitk` (build `work/build/w37`, 18:17 to 18:19, all under the GPU lock; full table
of run 1: `2026-11-03-hc-splitk-probe-run1.txt`). The three runs differ by at most 0.3 us per variant (the number below
is the mean). The P = 1 variant of the new kernel is bit-identical to `k_probe` (0 results differ), so the kernel is the
same code path. It is 0.5 to 2 us faster than `k_probe` (22.1 and 21.5 us) because it has no task table; the gain is
therefore always read against P = 1 of the same kernel.

Expectation was 22.6 us and 20.9 us today: 22.1 us (-2 %) and 21.5 us (+3 %) measured, inside the tolerance.

Deviation (fixed-order sum of the parts against the P = 1 sum; nb = 1): largest difference 0.96e-7 to 1.4e-7 of the
largest |y| (for all P and L), expected 1e-7 to 1e-6; 70 to 80 % of the results differ in the last bits (float
reassociation), none by more. The rows of nb = 4 at 320 x 10,240 print "inf" and 1,280 differing results: the reference
of `k_probe` is invalid there (zeros), not the new kernel.

Against the decision rule (less than 3 us per call: stop): gr.down with its real x source gains 3.1 us at P = 5 (2.5 at
P = 2), gr.up 1.7 to 1.8 us. Both are at or under the threshold. Added up for one gated residual the gain is 4.3 to
4.9 us, times 97 per pass = 0.42 to 0.48 ms (1.8 to 2.0 % of 23.7 ms) at B = 1, if all of it survived in the pass and
if reading the partial sums costs nothing; **both ungeprueft**. That is inside the ticket's guess (-0.3 to -0.6 ms) at
its lower half. The probe does not contain the inject of gr.down (4 x 10,240 BF16 on a few waves), which is why blocks
0 to 21 are slower than blocks 22 to 31 in the pass; how the units of the inject sit next to the units of the parts
is open.

Why P = 5 and P = 2 are good and P = 4, 8 and 10 are not (the units column of the table; **explanation not yet
verified**, the data are consistent with it): P = 5 gives 64 sub-blocks per part = 2 per lane = exactly one load round
of G = 2, 1,600 units (3.1 per wave on 512 waves). P = 4 gives 80 sub-blocks = 2.5 per lane (lanes 0 to 15 do three,
the others two: two rounds, lanes unequal). P = 8 and 10 have 40 and 32 sub-blocks per part but 2,560 and 3,200 units
(5 to 6 sequential units per wave), where the fixed cost per unit (address setup, reduction over 32 lanes) outweighs
the shorter chain.

## Implementation (block 2): what the switch does

`IE_HC_SPLITK=1` (default off; `IE_HC_SPLITK_PARTS` overrides the 5 parts, used on the mini model whose rows have 32
sub-blocks):
- gr.down: `DevTask::parts` = 5. Unit = (row, part), 32 lanes, `units_loop_q8_split` runs `row_q8` over the part's range
  of sub-blocks and writes the partial sum to `lo_part[token][part][row]` without the silu epilogue.
- gr.up (quantize-on-load, `matvec_quantize_x`): adds the 5 parts in the order 0..4, applies the same silu
  (`act_silu`, shared with `epilogue`) and quantizes. gr.up itself runs with 4 lanes per row instead of 2.
- Open spot from the report, checked in the code: `wide` is set only for tasks with at least `IE_WIDE_ROWS` (4096) rows
  (`ProgramBuilder::add`). gr.down (320 rows) is never wide, so its partial sums are valid in every pass. gr.up (10,240
  rows) is wide, and `wide_loop` reads x as float `lo`. So in a wide pass the consumer writes the final values to `lo`
  as well (`bt.wide`, the same variable that selects the wide path in `run_matvec`, in the same function; no second copy
  of the condition). A task with parts > 1 is never marked wide (guard in `add`, for `IE_WIDE_ROWS` below 320).
- No barrier, wait or dependency table changes: only the unit count of one task, its row/part mapping and the read in the
  quantize step.

Mini model (IE_HC_SPLITK_PARTS=4; the golden references are recorded with the switch off):
- Switch off: `golden.sh compare` (csv, rust, explain) and `golden-server.sh compare` (8 cases) identical to the
  references. (`work/build/w37`)
- Switch on: the three golden texts differ (expected: other float sums); two runs with `IE_DETERMINISTIC=1` give the same
  SHA; `--batch-check 300` (passes of 4 against single steps): 300 of 300 positions bit-identical, as with the switch off.
  `--check 4` against the CPU reference: top-1 equal in 4 of 4, worst relative deviation 2.49e-2 (off: 2.75e-2).
- Wide pass (`IE_WIDE_MIN=2 IE_WIDE_ROWS=512`, so that only gr.up is wide): top-1 equal 359 of 400 (off: 362), worst
  deviation 1.34e-1 (off: 1.42e-1). The wide path is lossy already without the switch; the write-back of `lo` works (the
  numbers would be garbage otherwise).
- No hang, no reset.

## Plan: step profile on the big model (block 3)

Question: how much shorter are gr.down, gr.up and the pass at B = 1 with `IE_HC_SPLITK=1`, and do blocks 0 to 21 of gr.down
still take longer than the rest (the inject)?

Scope: `ie-gpu-gen --mega`, coder IQ1_M, prompt csv of `scripts/bench-decode.sh` in the chat format, 200 tokens, no drafter
(every pass B = 1), as in 2026-11-02-mega-steps.md; build `work/build/w37` (this branch, the switch off is the default
code path). Four runs `IE_MEGA_PROF=1`, alternating off / on / off / on; then two runs with `IE_BAR_PROF` (off, on) for the
per-block times and the waiting at the barriers (`scripts/barprof.py`). All under the GPU lock, no build during the
runs, `systemd-run` with `MemoryMax=44G`, `IE_DIAG=1`, `IE_MEGA_INFO=1`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=6`, `IE_EXPERT_PROFILE`.

Expectation (source: probe above, `97 x` per pass): gr.down 1.885 ms -> 1.58 to 1.68 ms (-0.2 to -0.3 ms), gr.up 1.68 ms
-> 1.52 ms (-0.16 ms), pass 23.7 ms -> 23.2 to 23.4 ms (tolerance +-0.15 ms on the sum). Abort rule of the coordinator: a
gain below 0.25 ms per pass in the profile ends the work. Pass-to-pass spread of the profile: 0.1 ms (23.85 / 23.71 on
11-02).

Active tooling: `IE_MEGA_PROF=1` (all runs), `IE_BAR_PROF=<file>` (the last two only, costs a time stamp per step).

Switch on: gr.down **+150 us**, gr.up **+50 us**, pass **+0.17 to +0.26 ms** (23.56 -> 23.73 / 23.82; BAR_PROF 23.59 -> 23.79). Expected
was -0.2 to -0.3 ms for gr.down, -0.16 for gr.up, pass -0.35 to -0.45 ms. Far outside the expectation, and a gain of
less than 0.25 ms is the abort rule: **discarded** (the code stays on the branch behind the switch, not for merging).

Per block (`barprof.py`, us per step of gr.down, B = 1):
- off: blocks 0 to 3 17.5, blocks 4 to 15 15.0, blocks 16 to 19 17.9, blocks 20/21 17.1, blocks 22 to 31 7.9 (11-02: 15-18 / 9).
- on (5 parts, 4 lanes): blocks 0 to 3 16.6, **blocks 4 and 5 19.7**, blocks 6 to 31 16.1 to 16.6. The load is even now (the
  idle blocks 22 to 31 work), but every block needs 16 us, and blocks 4 and 5 are the slowest: the active inject units
  (nb = 1: 4 of its 32 units) sit on waves 64 to 95 behind the down units (units 1600 to 1631), and those waves also do 4 down
  units.
- The three mapping variants I tried afterwards (temporary env knobs, not committed): gr.up with 2 lanes instead of 4:
  pass 23.91 ms (BAR_PROF 23.76), gr.down 2,026 us; inject before the down task (units first): pass 23.99, blocks 0 and 1
  at 20.3 us (the inject on top of 3 down units), gr.down 2,078 us; both: 23.97 ms; switch off with inject first: pass
  23.80 (BAR_PROF 23.67), gr.down 1,948 us. None is better than the base (23.56 / 23.59 ms).

Reading (data-based, the cause is not proven): in the pass gr.down moves 3.5 MB in 16 us = 215 GB/s per block-balanced step;
the probe reaches 211 GB/s at P = 5 (3.48 MB / 16.5 us), so both sit at the same bandwidth. In the pass the unsplit step
was already at 15.0 us for most blocks; only 8 blocks (0 to 3, 16 to 21) at 17.5 to 17.9 us were above that, so there is
only ~2.5 us of the critical path to win, not the 3.1 us of the probe (which compared against a kernel without the inject
and without the 20 busy blocks of the pass). With the split the critical path is the inject block (19.7 us). Added to that
come the costs of the consumer: gr.up quantize-on-load reads 5 partial sums per value and computes the silu
(+50 us per pass, +0.5 us per call).

What would still be open (not built): a mapping that puts the inject on idle waves of otherwise less loaded blocks, or a
different inject (4 rows x 10,240 BF16 takes ~20 us on one block and is on the critical path in every variant). That is a
separate lever on the inject (also visible in the unsplit pass: blocks 16 to 21 at 17.1 to 17.9 us), worth a ticket of its
own; the ceiling for it is the 2.5 us per call between the 17.9 us blocks and the 15 us blocks, 0.24 ms per pass at most.

Not run because of the abort rule: `ab.py` decode on bench-set code/text, determinism of the big model (B = 1 against B = 4,
two runs) and `just cross-check` with the switch on. The mini model already showed the determinism: 300 of 300 positions
bit-identical between passes of 4 and single steps.
