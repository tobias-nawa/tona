## Finding before B0: Bolt prefill of more than one chunk failed (bug of round 1, fixed here)

The first B0 start (server, `prose` workload) served the 1845-token prompt (cold prefill 1.3 s = 1420 tok/s, decode 42.3 tok/s, 400 tokens) and then failed on the 16000-token
prompt with `hipGetLastError(): invalid configuration argument` (HTTP 500). Diagnosis with `AMD_LOG_LEVEL=3` on `tona-bolt-bench --prompt 16000` (`/var/tmp/claude/bolt2/d0.err`): the failing
launch is `k_scatter` with grid {0,1,1}; 160 earlier scatters (20 layers x 8 chunks of 2048 tokens) were fine. Cause: a prompt of more than one chunk (2048 tokens) is
prefilled layer by layer, and `PrefillRun::borrow_slots` takes the expert slots of the last layers as workspace and resets their `LayerCache` (`LC = LayerCache{}`, so `n_used` = 0);
`moe_latent` (Bolt) read `ne = M.cache_[l].n_used`, so the scatter of the first borrowed layer had `n * ne * 32 / 256 = 0` blocks. The Coder takes its `ne` from the dims (`D.ne`), so it was
not affected. Fix (`engine/gpu/arch/nemotron_h/moe_prefill.cpp`): `ne = M.m_.expert_used(l)`, the same value `mega_build_bufs.cpp` stores in `n_used`. Prompts up to one chunk do
not borrow slots and run the same code as before (bit-equal); every longer prompt crashed before, so there is no earlier output to keep equal. The D0 job above is the diagnosis (not a speed run).

So the B0 baseline below is "round 1 plus this fix". It also means that no Bolt prompt above 2048 tokens could have run on `main` before this round.

## P2 plan: the existing FP8 dense route on Bolt (item 3, the cheap probe of the dense-GEMM lever)

Prior art searched 2026-11-19 (topics: MoE and quantized GEMM on RDNA4, fused dequantization, int8 and FP8 WMMA): llama.cpp PR #17156 "HIP: WMMA-MMQ kernels for RDNA 4" (int8 WMMA,
`__builtin_amdgcn_wmma_i32_16x16x16_iu8_w32_gfx12`, activations quantized to 8 bit, weights unpacked as integers: no f16 copy; the established route for 4-bit weights; PR #17495 is the RDNA3 backport),
the GPUOpen WMMA guide part 2 (wide-K 16x16x32 from two 16x16x16 so that 8-bit operands use 128-bit loads), ROCmForge and VulkanForge (hand-written RDNA4 WMMA prefill kernels, community projects,
parity with llama.cpp at pp2048), llama.cpp PR #21344 (compute-bound MoE prefill on gfx1151, +8 to +24 %). Nothing found that fuses dequantization into a dense GEMM and beats unpack-once-and-share
(Tona's layered prefill unpacks every dense weight once per layer for all chunks of a group). What exists in Tona: `TONA_PREFILL_FP8=1` (dense weights to E4M3 with a factor per row, activations to E4M3 with a factor
per token, FP8 WMMA at twice the f16 rate; Ornith measured it as a quality loss on 4-bit weights, not measured for Bolt). NVFP4 values are integers (0 to 12) times a scale, so an int8 route would be exact on the weights.

## A1: LiLiCorr, what it is and what it would take (item 5, research, no run)

Sources read 2026-11-19: NVIDIA's LiLiCorr page and paper (arXiv 2608.20530, research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/lilicorr/), the Bolt model card, and the local GGUF
`NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-74B-A9B-24GB-LiLiCorr.gguf` (`tools/gguf_info.py`). Findings:

- **Format** (GGUF, arch `dflash`, "Li5_SWA", 1.1 B parameters, 0.93 GiB: 25 NVFP4 tensors 0.46 GiB, 30 BF16 0.41 GiB, 98 F32): 5 layers, width 4096, FFN 12288, 32 heads / 2 KV heads of 128, sliding window 1024 on all layers,
  non-causal inside a block, **block size 8**, conv kernel 2 (groups of 16), features from 6 target layers (2, 19, 36, 52, 69, 86); LiLiCorr head: 2 layers, width 1024, 8 heads, MLP 2048, **top 8 candidates per position**, logit scale 8.
  The drafter proposes 8 positions in one pass; the LiLiCorr head scores the lattice of 8 candidates per position and a greedy left-to-right walk picks one chain, which the target verifies in one pass of width up to 8.
- **Acceptance** (paper and model card, NVIDIA's numbers, not reproduced here): +9 to +19 % acceptance length over the plain DFlash drafter; for Bolt with 7 drafted tokens mean acceptance length 4.68 (greedy) and 4.25 (sampled) on a benchmark
  average at concurrency 64 in vLLM on the bf16/NVFP4 model. The paper names writing and roleplay as the high-entropy, hard-to-draft categories and gives no number for them. Our target is the IQ2 quantization (different weights than the
  drafter was trained against), so the acceptance here is lower than NVIDIA's; no measurement exists.
- **Licence**: OpenMDW-1.1, redistributable with licence and notices (`data/modeldb`); no obstacle.
- **What the engine needs** (nothing of it exists for this arch): the drafter model (`model/dflash_lilicorr.*`: 5 attention layers with sliding window and conv, NVFP4 and BF16 matvecs: reuse of the Coder's DFlash2 pieces is
  partial, the shapes and the conv differ), taps of the target's hidden state at 6 layers for every accepted token (the decode passes and the chunked prefill write them: an op after the layers 2, 19, 36, 52, 69, 86), the drafter's own
  windowed KV, the LiLiCorr scoring head and its walk, and the verify pass of width 8 with the Mamba replay commit (exists: B4, widths 1 to 8, `commit(m)`).
- **Cost model** (the decision input): rate = tau / (T_draft + T_pass(W)) against 1 / T_pass(1) now (24 ms). T_draft is the drafter's read of 0.93 GB plus a few launches, about 4 to 6 ms (a lower bound: 3 ms at 315 GB/s). T_pass(8) is the
  measurement D1 (planned above, not run yet, see the status line at the end of this file). Break-even tau = (T_draft + T_pass(8)) / 24 ms.

### A1 decision: LiLiCorr on prose (item 5)

Rate with a block of W verified tokens and a per-position acceptance probability p (chain acceptance, tau(W) = (1 - p^W) / (1 - p) tokens per pass), drafter time T_d (0.93 GB of weights: 3 ms at the VRAM bound, 4 to 6 ms with the launches; 5 ms used):
rate = tau(W) / (T_d + pass(W)); today 41.1 tok/s (1 / 24.35 ms).

| p | tau(4) | rate W=4 (T 41.8 ms) | tau(8) | rate W=8 (T 59.9 ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
