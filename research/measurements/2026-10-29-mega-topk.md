# Decode at long context: block selection and indexer score in the megakernel (#74, 2026-10-29)

Question: the time of a decode pass grows with the context (8K 45 ms, 128K 52 ms, 250K 63 ms per pass, MTP, ~2.9 tokens
per pass), and #73 showed that nearly all of the growth is the indexer of the 12 QSA layers (`op_idx_score` and
`op_topk`). How much of it goes away with bit-identical replacements?

Three steps (all three give bit-identical scores, cells and `n_cells`):

1. `op_topk` (`engine/gpu/mega_op_topk.h`) runs the three-pass radix of the prefill (`engine/gpu/topk_dev.h`, shared
   with `prefill_topk.hip`: 11/11/10 bits, LDS histogram in `sm`, wave scan, ballot output) instead of 32 one-bit passes
   with serial scans by thread 0. Threshold and tie rule are unchanged.
2. `op_idx_score` (`engine/gpu/mega_op_idx.h`): one wave per pool block scores all tokens of the pass (the pool of 32 MB
   per layer at 250K is read once, not once per token); four pool blocks in flight per wave; q of a token in registers
   for those four; the per-(token, block) 64-bit divisions are done once per step; the four head sums use 10 lane
   exchanges instead of 20 (reduce-scatter in the first two butterfly stages: the same additions in the same tree).
3. Several blocks per token for `op_topk`: not built, see below.

## Several blocks per token: not built

`op_topk` still takes 147 us per layer at 250K with one block per token (1.8 ms of the 47 ms per pass; 8 loads in flight
instead of 4 brought 154 -> 147 us, so it is not the latency of the scores; the likely limit is the contention of the
LDS atomics in the histogram of the first pass, where scores of one wave fall into few bins). Splitting a token over
several blocks needs a global exchange between the radix passes, i.e. 3 grid barriers (~1 us each in
2026-10-25-barriers, more with skew), or a two-step variant: each block selects its n_top best of its slice with the
same radix (ties earlier first), a barrier, then one block merges the S x 512 candidates by (score, index). That is
exact (every cell of the global selection is among the n_top best of its slice) and would save at best ~100 us per
layer, i.e. ~1.2 ms of 47 ms per pass (2.5 %), for a new op, a candidate buffer in the builder (`emit_attention`, which
#73 also changes) and an emit variant that maps candidate positions to block indices. Left as a follow-up: the gain is
below the spread of one round.
