# Expert overlap of two conversations (stage 1)

Own measurement, 2026-10-26. Two independent coding conversations (bench-effort `wrap` and `csv`, high, sampling), one
server start each with `IE_ROUTE_LOG`; evaluation `scripts/route-overlap.py` (the last row per position, so rejected
drafts drop out). 48 MoE layers, 10 experts per token.

Distinct experts per layer and pass (mean over all layers):

| Pass | k = 3 | k = 4 |
|---|---:|---:|
| one conversation, k tokens (today) | 22.0 | 26.8 |
| one conversation, 2k tokens (wider draft) | 35.1 | 42.3 |
| **two conversations, k tokens each** | **39.1** | **46.7** |
| without any overlap | 60 | 80 |

- Two independent conversations in one pass touch only ~10 % more experts than one conversation with the same number of tokens
  (46.7 against 42.3). Consecutive tokens of one conversation thus share hardly more experts than foreign ones: the routing
  is skewed, popular experts are needed everywhere.
- So a shared pass with 2 × 4 tokens should be hardly more expensive than a pass with 8 tokens of one conversation
  (~54–65 ms, measurements/2026-10-25-width-8), instead of 2 × 39 ms one after the other. Expectation: ~+30–40 % throughput with two
  workers. Not measured: hit rate of the expert cache when two conversations want different experts over a longer time.
