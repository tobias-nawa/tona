# Mac: why the second 4-token MMA chunk of a W8 pass saves less than the first (#108)

M1 Max 32 GB. With `TONA_METAL_MMA` every 4-token chunk of a pass runs its dense mat-vecs on the half MMA kernel
(`MV_BMAX = 4`, `engine/backend/metal/pass.mm`, `Pass::mv`). A chunk saved 6.7 to 7.2 ms at W4 to W7, but W8 (two
chunks) saved 11.7 ms, so its second chunk about 4.5 to 4.8 ms
([2026-11-12-mac-mma-pass-costs.md](2026-11-12-mac-mma-pass-costs.md)). That was inside the planned band for W8
(−10 to −16 ms) and not explained. Coordinator 2026-11-12: plan, profile, fresh search, this file.

## Hypotheses

- **H1, cache:** the two chunks of one matrix run back to back. The f32 kernel of chunk 2 finds the weights of chunk
  1 still in the system level cache (48 MB on the M1 Max, more than most single dense matrices of a layer), so the f32
  chunk 2 is cheaper than chunk 1 and the MMA kernel has less to save there. Predicts: f32 dense time per part at W8
  below 2× W4; MMA dense time at W8 near 2× W4.
- **H2, overlap:** without a barrier between the two dispatches (they write different rows of `y`) the GPU may run
  them together; the f32 kernel (128 threads per group, many groups) fills the machine less well alone than the MMA
  kernel (256 threads, 8 rows per group), so f32 gains more from the overlap. Predicts the same signature as H1 in
  the profile; separable only by a probe with a barrier between the chunks or with a cold cache.
- **H3, noise:** the difference (−11.7 against 2 × −7.0) is 2.3 ms; the spread between two runs of an arm was up to
  2.0 ms. Predicts: the profile shows a saving per part at W8 of 2× W4 within the spread.

## Plan (written before the run)

- Scope: `tona-bench DMT -b W -p 64 -d 64 -r 2 --ctx 4096` with `TONA_METAL_PROFILE=1` (GPU time per part, one
  command buffer per part) for W = 4 and 8, each with `TONA_METAL_MMA=0` and `=1`; order 4/off, 4/on, 8/off, 8/on,
  then the same again in reverse (8 runs, about 8 minutes). Model DM-T (the Mac's file), build `f6c74ce` (no engine
  change since `4d38665`). Nothing else on the GPU; quiet check and `hw_report` before and after.
- Read: per part, saving S(W) = off − on. The dense parts with MMA kernels are `attn_proj`, `dn_proj`, `gated_read`,
  `shared_exp` (`router` has < 2048 rows, `head` is excluded). Per part and in sum: S(8) / S(4) and the f32 ratio
  off(8) / off(4) for the same parts.
- Expectation: S(4) summed over the dense parts 6.5 to 7.5 ms per pass (the pass costs). H1 or H2: S(8) / S(4)
  1.5 to 1.8 and off(8) / off(4) below on(8) / on(4) for the large parts; H3: S(8) / S(4) 1.8 to 2.2.
- Active tooling: `TONA_METAL_PROFILE=1` (changes the buffer structure: one buffer per part, so overlap between parts
  is gone; overlap between the two chunks of one part stays), `TONA_METAL_MMA` per arm.
- Next step by outcome: H1 or H2 → an 8-token MMA kernel (one dispatch, the full 8×8 fragment, weights read and
  decoded once for 8 tokens) is the candidate; first a probe of its cost against two 4-token dispatches. H3 → note it
  and close the question.

## Result against the plan

- S(4) of the dense parts 8.0 ms per pass, above the expected 6.5 to 7.5 (profile mode: a buffer per part, no
  overlap between parts; the pass costs measured 7.2 at W4 without it).
- S(8) / S(4) = 1.82, the border between H1/H2 (1.5 to 1.8) and H3 (1.8 to 2.2). In the dense parts the second
  chunk saves 6.6 ms against 8.0 for the first: 1.4 ms of the 2.3 ms gap of the pass costs. The other 0.9 ms are at
  the size of the "other parts" line (+0.62 / −0.14 ms per pass, parts MMA does not touch), i.e. noise.
- **H1 (f32-specific cache) is not supported:** where the second chunk is cheaper (`gated_read`, `shared_exp`: about
  10 to 18 % below the first), it is cheaper for the f32 and the MMA kernel alike (off and on ratios equal per part).
  The saving shrinks because both chunks of these small matrices get cheaper in the same proportion, not because f32
  profits alone. `attn_proj` and `dn_proj`, the large matrices, scale 2.0 in both kernels.
- Answer: the second chunk of W8 saves less mainly because the second dispatch on the same small matrix is cheaper
  for both kernels (warm caches or overlap of the two dispatches; not separated, both kernels alike), plus about 1 ms
  of run-to-run noise in the pass costs. Nothing to fix in the 4-token kernel.

## Next step

The lever is not the second chunk's saving but the second chunk itself: at W8 the MMA kernel decodes every weight
twice, once per 4-token dispatch, and in each dispatch columns 5 to 8 of its 8×8 fragment repeat column 4. One
dispatch for up to 8 tokens (B a template parameter of `mvm`, `kernels/matvec_mma.metal`) decodes the weights once
and fills the fragment; the same kernel takes W5 to W7 in one dispatch instead of a 4-token MMA chunk plus a 1- to
3-token f32 chunk. Output column j depends on input column j only, so its rows should be bit-equal to the 4-token
kernel's (to be checked; then the quality check of the 4-token kernel covers it). Prior art: llama.cpp #29869
("metal : few-row MMA mat-mul", kernels for 2 to 16 rows on 8×8 simdgroup matrices, from b11404); the verify cost
that grows linearly with the batch on llama.cpp's Metal mat-vec path below 8 rows is measured in "Lossless but Not
Free" (arXiv 2607.17283, Sect. 6.5). MLX keeps its qmv kernels up to about 10 rows (ml-explore/mlx #3863, #3553).
Upper bound of the gain at W8: the second chunk's MMA dense time, on(8) − on(4) ≈ 40 to 50 ms per pass in this profile
(includes prefill; a probe decides).

## Probe: one MMA dispatch for up to 8 tokens

Change (uncommitted at the time of this plan): `mvm` takes the token count from `MvParams.tokens` (1 to 8) instead
of the constant 4; `Pass::mv` sends every rest of 4 or more tokens to one MMA dispatch of up to 8 tokens when
`TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE=1` (W5 to W8: one dispatch per matrix; W4 unchanged).

**Bit-equality (done before the plan below):** `tona-width-nll DMT --env X -n 400 -w 8 --ctx 4096
engine/backend/metal/pass.mm`, once with `TONA_METAL_MMA=1` (two 4-token dispatches) and once with
`TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE=1` (one 8-token dispatch): the 400 output lines (nll of A and B, KL, top-1 per token) are
byte-identical, with B different from A (the MMA path is active). The W8 rows of the wide kernel equal those of the
checked 4-token kernel. For W5 to W7 the tokens past the fourth move from the f32 kernel to the same MMA arithmetic.

### Plan (written before the run)

- Scope: `tona-bench DMT -b W -p 512 -d 64 -r 3 --ctx 4096` (as the pass-cost runs) for W = 4 to 8, A B B A per width
  with A = default (4-token chunks), B = `TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE=1`; 20 runs, about 12 minutes. Build of the change above
  on `795b61d`. Quiet check and `hw_report` before and after; nothing else on the GPU.
- Expectation (B − A per pass), from the profile above (the second chunk's MMA dense time ~40 to 50 ms at W8 with
  prefill, the 8-token dispatch costing a little more than a 4-token one) and the f32 table (W5 − W4 = 32 ms, of which
  the 1-token f32 dense rest is the larger part): W4 0 ± 1 ms; W5 −10 to −25; W6 −12 to −30; W7 −15 to −35;
  W8 −25 to −45 ms. Wide bands: the cost of the 8-token dispatch is not measured yet.
- Active tooling: `TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE=1` in B only; MMA on in both (default).
- Use: if B is faster, the costs go into a pass-cost table for the wide path and a server A/B decides the default.

## Probe 2: several 8-row tiles per threadgroup

Cause looked for in the W8 finding, by arithmetic before measuring: every threadgroup of `mvm` (8 rows) reads all of
`x` for its K. For a 4096 × 2048 matrix that is 512 threadgroups × 2048 × B × 4 bytes: 16.8 MB at B = 4 and 33.6 MB at
B = 8, against 6.9 MB of Q6_K weights. The input reads, not the weights, dominate, and they double from B = 4 to 8.
Change: `mvm<T, NR>` keeps NR row tiles per threadgroup (an accumulator each) and loads each input fragment once for
all NR (`TONA_METAL_MMA_ROWS` = 1, 2, 4; kernels `mvm_`, `mvm2_`, `mvm4_`). Each tile sums in the same order as
before. Bit-equality: the `tona-width-nll -w 8` output of NR = 2 and NR = 4 is byte-identical to NR = 1.

### Plan (written before the run)

- Scope: `tona-bench DMT -b W -p 512 -d 64 -r 3 --ctx 4096`, all arms with `TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE=1`; A NR = 1, B NR = 2,
  C NR = 4; order A B C C B A per width, W = 4 to 8 (30 runs, about 8 minutes). Quiet check (ok at 00:05:17) and
  `hw_report` before and after.
- Expectation: input traffic halves (B) or quarters (C). If the ~7 ms of the 5-to-8-token dispatch over the 4-token one
  is the input traffic, B saves 3 to 5 ms at W5 to W8 and 2 to 3 ms at W4, C 4 to 7 ms at W5 to W8; fewer
  threadgroups (256 or 128 per 4096 rows across 32 GPU cores) may cost occupancy, so C can lose against B.
- Active tooling: `TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE=1` in all arms, `TONA_METAL_MMA_ROWS` per arm.

### Run (00:08 to 00:17): invalid

All arms were far slower than before (NR = 1 at W4 146 ms against 83.7 in probe 1). Cause: the first form of the
change loaded the four input fragments of a step into an array of `simdgroup_half8x8` before the tiles; that form ran
the default 4-token MMA pass at 145 ms (f32: 91 ms), so the kernel itself had become slow, for every NR. The run is
discarded (`work/`); the numbers say nothing about the row tiles.

other mat-vecs.** The same limit is in every W5 to W8 pass with or without the wide kernel.

## Result of this file

1. The second MMA chunk of W8 saves less because the second dispatch on the same small matrix is cheaper for both
   kernels (profile 1); nothing to fix there.
2. One MMA dispatch for up to 8 tokens (`TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE`, `cfd7475`) is bit-equal to the 4-token chunks at W8
   and makes W5 to W8 12 to 19 ms faster; default after a pass-cost table and a server A/B.
3. The rest of the W5 step is the 4-token limit of the expert, head and narrow mat-vecs; candidate for its own
   change (kernels for 5 to 8 tokens, as the MMA kernel now has).
