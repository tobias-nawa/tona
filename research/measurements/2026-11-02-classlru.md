# ClassLRU in the server: why the simulation and the server disagreed, and the A/B, 2026-11-02 (#98)

## Step 1: the gap between simulation and server (no GPU)

Question (#98): LRFU was +0.5 / +1.0 points in the simulation and brought nothing in the server
(2026-10-24-misses.md, "Decaying frequency instead of LRU"). Can ClassLRU, +0.76 / +1.12 points at 90 slots in
2026-11-02-cache-admission-sim.md, bring anything there either?

What the server does that the simulation did not model, with the code that does it:

1. **Missed experts do not enter the cache.** The simulation puts every missed expert of the union into the cache
   (`tools/cache_sim/policies.py` `LruCache.demand`, `LrfuCache.demand`; `classlru.py` `ClassLruCache.demand`, the
   loop over `sorted(misses)`). The server does this only while slots are free: `choose_fill`
   (`engine/gpu/mega_select_cpu.cpp:174-175`) refills `free_slots.empty() ? fill_n_ : min(fill_cap_, free)`, and
   `fill_n_` is `IE_FILL`, default 0 (`mega_build_bufs.cpp:225`), `fill_cap_` = 4 (`:235`). Since 2026-10-24 the
   cache therefore changes in steady state only by (a) preloading, 8 experts per layer and pass at the back of the
   list (`mega_prefetch.cpp:183-207`), and (b) promotion of hits (`mega_select_cpu.cpp:27-30`). The README says the
   same ("decode no longer fills misses into the cache in addition"), the simulation note of 2026-10-25 did not
   carry it over.
2. **Ramp-up**: while slots are free, misses and preloaded experts fill them (`mega_cache.h:27-30`, free slots first,
   then at most 4 misses per layer and pass). The simulation counts from 20 % of the run, the server's `/status`
   counters cover the whole request including the ramp-up (the `ab.py` counters are per request, prefill and decode
   together).
3. **Timing of the fill**: a refilled expert sits in its slot one pass later (`install_pending`,
   `mega_select_cpu.cpp:46-62`); the simulation inserts it at once. Small, because at most 4 per layer and pass enter,
   and only during ramp-up.
4. **Host cost of the policy.** Not modelled at all. The old LRFU (`git show 65a24b1`, `Mega::pick_victim` and
   `lrfu_score`, removed in a642290) scanned the whole `where` map of the layer (90 entries, one `exp2` each) for
   every victim; a victim is needed for each of the 8 preloaded experts per layer: 8 x 48 layers x 90 = about 35,000
   `exp2` and map visits per pass, about 0.3 to 1 ms of host time per pass (estimate, 10 to 30 ns per visit; not
   measured) next to a pass of 42 ms. The simulation's gain was 0.4 to 0.65 ms (0.65 ms per point of union hit rate,
   research/36 section 4.1). That is the same size, so "0 +- 0.5 %" is no contradiction.
5. **The earlier LRFU expectation was too high**: it was +2.1 to +2.4 points *without* preloading
   (2026-10-24-misses.md); with preloading it is +0.4 to +1.0 (2026-10-25-cache-policy.md). The server test of
   2026-10-24 (rust 76.1 / 76.2 against 76.7 / 76.6 tok/s, H = 10 default, 4 and 25) was made against the first
   number.

Not modelled and not a cause of a gap in hits: CPU/GPU split of the misses (it decides where a miss is computed, not
which experts the cache holds, except through `IE_FILL`), the prefetch moment (the candidates are chosen from the
prediction in the same pass in both), and the draft width (the trace holds `nb` per pass).

### The simulation with the server's fill rule (new: `tools/cache_sim/cli.py --server-fill`)

`--server-fill`: a missed expert enters only while slots are free, at most 4 per layer and pass (point 1). Same two
traces (`trp1.bin`, `trp2.bin`), one deterministic run each, hits over the unions from 20 % of the run, pairs are
trp1 / trp2 (difference to LRU in points).

| Slots | Rule | LRU | LRFU H = 10 | ClassLRU 4 / 128 | ClassLRU 8 / 128 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 90 | every miss in (old) | 91.87 / 90.12 | 92.38 / 91.08 | 92.63 / 91.24 | 92.56 / 91.23 |
| 90 | server (IE_FILL=0) | 89.43 / 88.23 | 90.08 / 89.26 | 89.74 / 89.21 | 89.68 / 89.22 |
| 90 | server, difference | | +0.65 / +1.03 | **+0.31 / +0.98** | +0.25 / +0.99 |
| 70 | every miss in (old) | 86.35 / 85.11 | 87.94 / 86.60 | 88.15 / 86.66 | 88.14 / 86.74 |
| 70 | server (IE_FILL=0) | 84.76 / 83.43 | 85.89 / 84.92 | 85.44 / 84.66 | 85.42 / 84.79 |
| 70 | server, difference | | +1.13 / +1.49 | **+0.68 / +1.23** | +0.66 / +1.36 |

Reading:

- The fill rule lowers LRU by 2.4 / 1.9 points (90) and 1.6 / 1.7 (70), and halves the gain of ClassLRU at 90 slots
  (+0.76 / +1.12 became +0.31 / +0.98) and at 70 (+1.80 / +1.55 became +0.68 / +1.23).
- The tuning does not move: 4 classes, half-life 128 tokens stays at the top or within 0.1 of it on both traces
  (full grid: `python3 tools/cache_sim/cli.py --slots 90 --server-fill --grid <traces>`).
- LRFU H = 10 is above ClassLRU under the server rule (+0.3 / +0.05 at 90, +0.45 / +0.26 at 70), and it gave nothing in
  the server. If the host cost of point 4 is the reason, ClassLRU, whose victim choice looks at four list ends
  instead of the whole layer, keeps what LRFU lost; if the reason is something else, ClassLRU gets nothing either.
  That is exactly what the A/B decides.

### Verdict on step 1

The gap is explained in part by a rule the simulation lacked (point 1), in part by a host cost that is plausible but
not measured (point 4). The remaining predicted gain is +0.3 to +1.0 points at 90 slots (0.2 to 0.65 ms per pass,
about +0.5 to +1.5 % decode, an estimate) and +0.7 to +1.2 points at 70 slots (about +1 to +2 %). That is not zero and
not below what `ab.py` can resolve (decode spread 0.2 to 0.6 %), so the stop condition of the ticket ("ClassLRU cannot
bring anything") is **not** met. The switch is built, with an O(1) victim choice (four list ends, no scan of the
layer), and judged by `ab.py`.

## Against the acceptance rule of the ticket

"Better at 70 slots, not worse at the high count." At 70 slots B is faster in `short` decode@2000 (decided), faster in
the point estimate in the other three metrics (2 of them 6 of 6 rounds), none worse. At 82 / 74 slots no metric is
slower; the point estimates are +0.1 to +0.5 %, intervals not excluding a small loss (lower bounds -0.85 to -0.04 %).
That is "better at 70" with one decided metric and seven positive undecided ones, and "not measurably worse" at the
high count. The effect is small (+0.3 to +0.6 % decode, 0.3 to 1.1 points of hits), at the lower end of what the
simulation allowed. ClassLRU stays a switch, default off; whether it becomes the default is for the maintainer to decide.
