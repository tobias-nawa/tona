# Mac: f32 mat-vecs for 5 to 8 tokens per dispatch (experts, LM head, narrow matrices)

Issue #108. Follows [2026-11-12-mac-w8-mma.md](2026-11-12-mac-w8-mma.md#where-the-step-w4--w5-goes): with the wide
MMA kernel on, about 11 ms of the step W4 → W5 (profile mode) sit in the kernels that still run in chunks of 4 tokens
(`MV_BMAX`): routed experts (`mvg`, slots of one expert in groups of 4), shared expert, gated reads (narrow matrices,
`mvs`) and the LM head (`mv`). At W5 each opens a second chunk of one token and pays a second pass over its weights.

## Plan (written before the runs)

**Change under test.** `TONA_METAL_MV8=1` (probe switch, off by default while probed): the f32 kernels `mv`, `mvs`
take up to 8 tokens per dispatch (instances B = 5 to 8 next to 1 to 4), `mvg` takes an expert's slots in groups of up
to 8. The arithmetic per token and row is unchanged (`rows_dot` keeps the tokens apart, same loop and order), so every
width stays bit-identical to width 1: check 2 must stay at 0 differing rows with the switch on.

**Prior art.** llama.cpp `mul_mv_ext` (ggml-metal, checked at d9ee007, `ggml-metal-ops.cpp:2640-2726`) runs 2 to 8
tokens through one dispatch but holds at most 4 or 5 tokens per threadgroup (`r1ptg`: 2, 3, 4, 5, 3, 4, 4 for 2 to 8
tokens) and spreads the rest over a second threadgroup column of the same dispatch; register pressure is the likely
reason. Variant B of this probe (only if A loses at W6 to W8) is that form: one dispatch, grid y = chunks of 4.

**Checks with the switch on.** Checks 1 to 4 on DM-T, check 1 on the mini model, unit tests.

**Speed run.** `tona-bench DMT -b W -p 512 -d 64 -r 3 --ctx 4096`, W = 4 to 8, arms A (`TONA_METAL_MV8=0`) and B
(`=1`) in the order A B B A per width, one build, quiet machine. 20 runs, about 25 min. Then, if B wins, a profile
(`TONA_METAL_PROFILE=1`, W = 5 and 8) for the parts.

**Expectation.** From the excess table: W4 unchanged (±1 ms, the same kernels run); W5 −4 to −10 ms (the second
chunk of one token goes away in experts, shared expert, gated reads and head; ~9.6 ms profile excess, the pass shows
less than profile mode); W6, W7 −3 to −10 ms; W8 −2 to −10 ms (two full chunks become one, which saves a weight pass
but holds 8 accumulators per row). A slower W6 to W8 means register spills (finding; then variant B).

**Active tooling.** None besides the switch; `TONA_METAL_MMA` and `TONA_METAL_MMA_WIDE` at their defaults (on); no
`TONA_METAL_SOURCES`, no profile during the speed run.

## Run 2 plan: at most 5 tokens per dispatch (variant C)

**Change under test.** `TONA_METAL_MV5=1`: dense f32 mat-vecs (`mv`, `mvs`) with at most 5 tokens per dispatch, the
pass split into as few even chunks as fit (5; 6 as 3+3; 7 as 4+3; 8 as 4+4, llama.cpp's split); experts unchanged.
`TONA_METAL_MV5=2`: also the routed experts with up to 5 slots per weight pass (`mvg5`, its own instance). The B = 6 to
8 instances and `mvg8` are removed. Check 2 with both values: 0 of 66 rows differ; check 1 at value 2: 39/39 at
1.40e-06 (the baseline).

**Speed run.** As run 1, arms 0, 1, 2 in the order 0 1 2 2 1 0 per width, W = 4 to 8: 30 runs, about 8 min.

**Expectation.** From the run-1 profile: value 1 W5 −2 to −4 ms (head, shared expert, gated reads), W4, W7, W8 ±1
(same chunks as now: 4, 4+3, 4+4), W6 ±1.5 (3+3 instead of 4+2); value 2 like value 1 plus whatever `mvg5` gains at W5
minus its register cost at every width; a cost at W4 above 1 ms rules it out.

Against the plan: value 1 at W5 −4.4 ms, just past the expected −2 to −4; W6 −1.3 (3+3 instead of 4+2) within ±1.5

**Kept: value 1** (dense f32 mat-vecs with at most 5 tokens per dispatch, evenly split). **Not kept: `mvg5`** — its
cost is the register count of a 5-slot weight pass, which is the change itself (nothing to remove); the slot limit of
the expert kernel stays 4. Another form that might lift it — an expert's slots over several threadgroups of one
dispatch instead of sequential passes in one — is a separate probe.

## Server A/B plan

`scripts/ab.py --workload set`, DM-T + MTP-164, same build, A defaults, B `TONA_METAL_MV5=1` (also the `mv5` cost
table), 2 to 6 rounds. Expectation (given to ab.py before the start): passes of 5 tokens about 4 % and of 6 about 1 %
cheaper, the others unchanged; at ~3.1 tokens per pass only a part of the passes is that wide: decode code +0 to +2 %,
text −0.5 to +1 %. Result: [2026-11-13-mac-mv5-server-ab.md](2026-11-13-mac-mv5-server-ab.md).

## Server A/B result

6 rounds (60 min), conditions: before warning (7.4 % other CPU), after "GPU 95 %" right at the end, ok 20 s later.
Decode code 36.7 → 37.6 tok/s, **+3.64 % [+0.79, +6.49], decided** after round 2; above the expected +0 to +2 %
(finding: the server's width choice moves with the cheaper W5/W6 costs as well, as with the wide MMA path). Decode
text 25.9 → 26.1, −0.20 % [−6.39, +5.99], **open** after 6 rounds; the per-round spread (−9.9 % to +5.5 %) is far
above any effect the pass costs predict (text passes are narrower). Both arms ran the same build; A in this run is
faster than A of the wide-MMA A/B (code 36.7 against 35.6 for the same configuration), a drift between sessions,
not between arms.

**Decision (maintainer, 2026-11-13):** on by default: code matters more, and −0.2 % on text is within his
tolerance. Text stays open after 6 rounds (no loss shown, no parity shown either). `TONA_METAL_MV5=0` brings the
4-token chunks back.
