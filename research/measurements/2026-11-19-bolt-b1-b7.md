### Expectation

- Real file loads: yes, expected without changes to the binders (the tensor names are in `model/blocks.cpp` already, written from the same header). Tolerance: none, it loads or it does not.
- Oracle: finite logits for all 64 steps of both texts. Rate: a token reads about 9 B active parameters, roughly 6 GB of quantized weights; with the row-wise dequantization to `double` I expect 10 to 60 s per token on 16 threads (estimate from the Ornith oracle, 3 B active; unverified for this file). 128 tokens then take up to two hours; the page cache holds the 22 GB file after the first pass.
- Top-1 hits of the teacher-forced text: not a gate here. A text the model predicts well should give clearly more than chance (above 20 %); I report the number and do not interpret it as quality.
- Tokenizer against llama.cpp: identical id sequences in both modes. The one known divergence of llama.cpp from the original Tekken regex (non-ASCII letters count as upper and lower case) is reproduced on purpose, so that the check is against llama.cpp.
- Jinja: all cases byte-equal.

### Active tooling

`TONA_DETERMINISTIC` not needed (CPU oracle). No profilers, no diagnostics beyond `tona-ref --hash` to make the two oracle runs comparable. `OMP_NUM_THREADS` default of the oracle.

### Stop rule

- Oracle runs: stop a run when a step produces non-finite logits (that is the answer), or when the first 4 tokens take more than 5 minutes per token (the oracle is then too slow for 128 tokens: switch to 32 tokens per text and say so), or after 3 h in total. Look at each running job at least every 15 minutes.
- Build or test failure: fix in the code, rebuild; no more than the one build directory.
- Tokenizer or chat mismatches: each is a bug to fix, no tolerance.

### Deviations from the plan, and things to know

- `llama-logits-dump --mode cpu` of the shared llama.cpp HIP build still created a small ROCm context (62 MiB compute
  buffer) for two runs of a few seconds each, outside the queue (coordinator: next time such tools go through
  `gpu_queue --kind gpu`). No GPU was needed for any check; I will use a CPU-only
  llama.cpp build for further cross-checks.
- `tools/gates/...` and `just check` are reported in the ticket report, not here.
- Pixtral follows Hugging Face's regex classes (the model was trained with that tokenizer), not llama.cpp's approximation
  (`[^a-z]` / `[^A-Z]` for non-ASCII): `aÉ` is two words here (and in the original regex), one in llama.cpp. Case
  tables from `tools/gen_case_tables.py` (Unicode 16). Differences from llama.cpp therefore appear only for a non-ASCII
  capital after a lower-case letter; the Hugging Face package was not available offline, the classes are the ones of
  Mistral's published pattern. The 250-text and corpus comparison above was made before this change (with llama.cpp's
  classes) and was not repeated.
- After merging main (Ornith O4/O5): `chat_vs_jinja` and `chat_tokens` take the family from `--model FILE`; ctest runs
  the Bolt and Ornith targets (all pass); `just test-cpu` 68 of 68; Coder goldens below.
- Coder goldens on the merged head (build `work/build/bolt-gpu`): `golden.sh compare` all identical (csv, rust, explain, ...); `golden-server.sh compare` every case identical, exit 0.
