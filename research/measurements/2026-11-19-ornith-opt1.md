## Plan

**Question 1 (decode).** Where does a pass of Ornith (Q4_K_M, experts partly in RAM) spend its time per width, and how far
is it from the bandwidth ceiling? Decision per result: a step family with more than 15 % of the pass whose achieved
bandwidth is below 60 % of the ceiling becomes the lever; a pass dominated by CPU experts (`gpu_direct`/`cpu` counts) is
a placement question (#148), not a kernel one, and is reported to the maintainer.

**Ceiling (bytes per pass, width 1, from `tools/gguf_info.py --tensors`, computed by hand, source for the expectation).**

| Part | Bytes |
|---|---|
| DeltaNet layer dense (qkv Q6_K 13.8 MB, gate 4.7, ssm_out 4.7, alpha/beta 0.1) x 30 | 23.3 MB x 30 = 700 MB |
| Attention layer dense (q 9.4, k 0.6, v 0.9, o 4.7) x 10 | 15.6 MB x 10 = 156 MB |
| Router (F32 2048 x 256) x 40 | 2.1 MB x 40 = 84 MB |
| Shared expert (gate, up Q4_K, down Q6_K) x 40 | 2.0 MB x 40 = 80 MB |
| LM head (Q6_K 2048 x 248320) | 417 MB |
| 8 routed experts per layer (0.59 + 0.59 + 0.86 MB each) x 40 | 16.3 MB x 40 = 653 MB |
| DeltaNet state read and write (32 x 128 x 128 x 4 B x 2) x 30 | 126 MB |
| **Total** | **about 2.2 GB** |

At 315 GB/s (docs/backends/reference-platform.md) this is 7.0 ms per pass, 143 passes/s, if every expert is in VRAM. The routed experts
are 20.9 GB of the 21.6 GB file, the VRAM holds about half of them, the rest comes from RAM (CPU or over PCIe, 72 and
55 GB/s). The ceiling therefore depends on the hit rate; the run reports hits, GPU direct and CPU experts per pass.
With MTP (about 3 tokens per pass in code, 2.1 in prose, `2026-11-19-ornith-tona-rust-go.md`) the ceiling is 3 x the pass
rate at the width where the experts of the union still fit.

**Scope (changed).** `tona-gpu-gen` is Coder-only (first attempt: "architecture qwen35moe instead of qwen4exp"). The profile
comes from the server instead: `TONA_MEGA_PROF=1 TONA_MEGA_PROF_EVERY=n` (new diagnostic, `engine/gpu/mega_serve.cpp`: the
step profile of the last n passes with the mean pass width, to stderr) and `TONA_MTP_PROF=1` (existing, the MTP program).
One server (port 8095, ctx 32768), 6 greedy requests of 400 tokens, once with `TONA_SPEC_OFF=1` (every pass width 1) and
once with the production path (MTP). Deterministic counters first: step shares per pass.

**Expectation.** Width 1: 63 to 89 tok/s measured earlier (11 to 16 ms per pass, server, no MTP); this run is a bare
pass, so 10 to 16 ms (tolerance +-30 %). A result outside is a finding. Source: `2026-11-19-ornith-tona-rust-go.md`.

**Active tooling.** `TONA_MEGA_PROF=1` (in-kernel step timestamps, adds a few us per step), no rocprof, no
`TONA_DETERMINISTIC`.

**Stop rules.** The run ends after the widths are done. A GPU fault, out-of-memory or queue exit 75 stops it and goes to the
maintainer. Jobs are looked at every 15 minutes at most.

**Why this size.** Profile shares are stable after a few dozen passes (the Coder's profiles used 100 passes); 300 passes
at three widths take about 10 s of GPU time plus the model load, which is the cost. No noise.toml entry exists for
`tona-gpu-gen`; the two repeats show the spread of one process.

## Plan of the changes (written after D1, before their runs)

Levers chosen from D1 (all in Ornith-owned code or its own files; all lossless for the output):

- **L1 truncated MTP head** (`engine/gpu/arch/qwen35moe/mtp.cpp`): the draft step reads 85 MB instead of 417 MB of head.
  Output distribution unchanged (the target verifies every draft); the acceptance may drop a little (1 to 5 % for the Coder).
  `TONA_MTP_VOCAB=full` restores the base.
- **L2 MTP prompt entries by GEMM** (`engine/gpu/arch/qwen35moe/prefill_mtp.cpp`): eh_proj, K and V of the MTP layer for
  the whole prompt chunk as f16 GEMMs instead of one megakernel pass per 8 tokens. Only K and V of the MTP layer enter the
  drafts. `TONA_MTP_PF_MEGA=1` restores the base.
- Not touched: shared prefill and attention files (B6 is wiring them), shared decode steps, expert placement (#148).

**Arms of every `ab.py` run:** A = `ornith-opt-base` (binaries of `ce18c48`), B = `ornith-opt` (this branch). Both start the
same Ornith Q4_K_M file with its embedded MTP head (`--drafter none`), ctx 32768, port 8095, memory limit 54G.

**M1 identity (R4 style, 1 round, `TONA_DETERMINISTIC=1`, workload `short`, 400 decode tokens).** Question: are the greedy
tokens the same in A and B? Expectation, stated before: **identical text** for 4 requests if the pass numerics do not depend on
the width (the Coder's bit-exact path is built for this), because L1 and L2 only change the drafts. If the texts differ the
cause is a width-dependent rounding of the Ornith pass that the drafts expose (a finding about the target path, not
about the drafts), and I check it by running A with `TONA_MTP_VOCAB=full` against A with `TONA_MTP_PF_MEGA=1` and a third
combination to see which change moves it. Stop at the first difference: no rounds are added.

**M2 speed, decode and prefill (`ab.py`, normal mode, workload `server`, targets `2000:400,16000:100`, ctx 32768).**
Expected B against A: decode **+5 to +12 %** (head 1.385 ms of 1.67 ms per draft step, about 2.5 steps per 21 ms cycle =
about 13 %, minus 1 to 5 % acceptance); prefill@2000 **+15 to +40 %** (about 250 MTP passes of 1.7 ms = 0.42 s of about 1.3 s),
prefill@16000 **+10 to +30 %**. Rounds: no noise.toml entry for Ornith, so at least 2, at most 4 (rule: two without a known
spread); ab.py alternates AB/BA. Why this size: 400 decode tokens at 2K context are the workload the Coder's decode numbers
use; 16K shows the MTP-prompt part at a prefill that is long enough to matter, without the attention drop (beyond 16K).

**D2 decode against the oracle (one `gpu` job, two `tona-check` runs, 100 steps, ctx 4096, 100 greedy tokens after a 20-token
prompt).** Question: is the deviation (relative logit difference up to 0.23, top-1 164 of 177 earlier) the oracle's precision
or ours? Run 1: oracle as it is. Run 2: `TONA_REF_Q8ACT=1` (oracle inputs rounded like the kernels'). Decision, fixed before:
if the maximum relative deviation of run 2 is **at most half** of run 1's and top-1 agreement rises to at least 95 %, the
deviation is the int8 activation rounding of the decode kernels (by design, the same as llama.cpp's), not an error: report
and leave it (removing it would be a numbers change, maintainer). If it stays within 25 % of run 1, the cause is something
else in the kernels and goes to the maintainer with the per-layer plan. Expectation: the first, because the prefill kernels
with f16 inputs were at 0.123 mean |d nll| against 0.167 for decode. One run each (deterministic computation on both
sides, no spread).

**C1 and C3 for the Coder (plan).** C1: the branch changes no `.hip` file and no header a `.hip` file includes (`mega.h` is
included by none), so no device object is rebuilt: the 51 `.hip.o` of the build are the ones of the base build (their
mtimes precede the first edit); `scripts/isa-diff.py` against a second full base build would compare identical objects and was
not run for that reason (stated, not hidden). C3: `golden.sh compare` (decode, cpu plan, prefill with the dflash drafter) and
`golden-server.sh compare`, in one `gpu` job (`/var/tmp/claude/ornith-opt/goldens.sh`), exit code of every script recorded,
the first failure stops the block. Expectation: all identical (the host changes are a diagnostic behind an environment
variable, a member, oracle switches that are off, and Ornith-only files).

Ceiling at width 1: 2.2 GB per pass is 7.0 ms (143 tok/s) if all experts were in VRAM; we are at 11.3 to 12.2 ms (60 %).
The gap of 4.3 to 5.2 ms is: CPU handshake per router 0.8 to 1.0 ms (host.wait) + 0.3 (moe.copy) + 0.15 (host.y); router
matvec 0.5 ms over its bandwidth time; the MoE steps 0.8 ms over the bytes of VRAM-resident experts (misses go over
PCIe/CPU); about 0.8 ms of small steps (barrier and launch per step, 600 steps per pass at 1 to 3 us). The dense matvecs
and the head are already at 80 to 100 % of the bandwidth: no kernel lever in dn.in, head.out.

Production path (MTP, same prompts): **108.0 to 117.7 tok/s**, 2.56 to 2.92 tokens per pass (3.5 to 3.75 with history
drafts), passes at mean width 3.2 to 4.0 take **17.2 to 20.3 ms** (moe.gate_up 4.2 ms, moe.down 3.3 ms, dn.in 2.5 ms).
**One MTP draft step takes 1,669 us, of which `mtp.head` 1,385 us (83 %)**: the step reads the full LM head (417 MB, Q6_K)
for one row's worth of draft. With about 2 draft steps per pass that is about 2.8 ms of a 21 ms token cycle (13 %).
Lever: the truncated MTP head the Coder already has (`TONA_MTP_VOCAB`, 41,500-id list plus 8,192 dynamic rows = 85 MB).

Deviation against the oracle, read in the code before running anything: `ref::matvec` computes with **F32 activations and
double dots** (`engine/cpu/ref_ops.cpp`), while the decode kernels quantize every matvec input to int8 in blocks of 32
(`wave_q8`, `engine/gpu/mega_op_common.h`). The decode path therefore differs from the oracle by this rounding by design (the
same one llama.cpp's MMVQ makes with Q8_1 inputs). Test of the hypothesis: `TONA_REF_Q8ACT=1` (added, off by default)
rounds the oracle's matvec inputs the same way; if the deviation of the decode path against that oracle falls clearly,
the cause is the activation rounding and not an error of the kernels (block D2 below).


### D2: decode against the oracle (job `ornith-opt-d2`, build `ornith-opt`, 20 prompt tokens, 100 generated, ctx 4096)

| Oracle | Steps | Top-1 equal | Worst relative deviation | Typical step (steps 100 to 119) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as is (F32 activations, double dots) | 120 | 119 | 0.120 (step 0) | 1.4e-2 to 3.5e-2 |
| `TONA_REF_Q8ACT=1` (matvec inputs rounded to int8 blocks of 32) | 120 | 116 | 0.101 | 1.5e-2 to 4.2e-2 |

Rule of the plan: a result at most half of run 1 would have shown the activation rounding as the cause. It is **not**
(0.101 against 0.120, top-1 lower, not higher): **the activation rounding of the decode kernels is not the source of the
deviation, and the oracle's F32 inputs are not either.** The deviation has two parts: a high value at step 0 (0.115, max |d| 2.05;
the first token of a sequence has the largest activations) and a level of 1.5 to 4 % of the largest logit on every later step,
with a few spikes (step 13: 0.089, step 23: 0.080), top-1 equal in 119 of 120. The earlier figure "0.23, 164 of 177" is of the
same kind (a few near-tie steps). Remaining candidates, not yet separated: the int8 KV cache (K and V in blocks of 32 in
the 10 attention layers, the oracle holds them in float), the DeltaNet recurrence in F32 against the oracle's double, and
different top-8 expert choices on near-ties of the router. Block D3 tests the KV candidate with `TONA_REF_Q8KV=1`
(added the same way). llama.cpp's run in the N7 table used a q8_0 KV cache too, which would explain why Tona agrees with it
(KL 0.0056) better than with the oracle.

### D3: decode against the oracle with the int8 KV cache emulated (job `ornith-opt-d3`, same prompt and settings as D2)

| Oracle | Top-1 equal (of 120) | Worst relative deviation |
|---|---|---|
| as is | 119 | 0.120 |
| `TONA_REF_Q8ACT=1` | 116 | 0.101 |
| `TONA_REF_Q8KV=1` (K and V rounded to int8 blocks of 32) | 118 | 0.131 |
| both | 118 | 0.086 |

By the rule fixed in the plan (at most half of 0.120) **neither the activation rounding nor the int8 KV nor both explain the
deviation** (0.086 is 72 % of 0.120; top-1 does not improve). The oracle's precision is not the cause either (F32 inputs,
double dots, weights dequantized exactly as the kernels read them). What stays: a level of 1.5 to 4 % of the largest logit at
every step with top-1 equal in 118 to 119 of 120 (the N7 comparison against llama.cpp-hip has Tona at KL 0.0056, 96.9 %
top-1 over 5,068 positions, i.e. the same kind of agreement). Not separated, in the order I would test: (1) top-8 expert
choices that flip on near-ties (the router input is `xn` as F32 in the kernel, the same in the oracle, but the sum before
it differs by rounding), (2) the DeltaNet recurrence and conv (F32 and fast math in the kernel against double), (3) the
attention softmax, (4) an error in a kernel. The decisive experiment needs a per-layer tap of the decode path against the
oracle's `Trace` (no tap exists for the decode path; the prefill file says the same): one `gpu` job after a small change to
the Ornith program that copies `R` of every layer to the host. I did not build it in this round (shared `Mega` code, and the
maintainer asked for lossless levers first); stated as the next step with a stop rule: the first layer whose relative
difference of `R` exceeds 1e-3 is the suspect. Until then the claim "decode matches llama.cpp, deviates from the oracle by
design" is **not proven**: the activation rounding by design is excluded as the main cause by this measurement.
